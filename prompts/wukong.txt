  1 · Singe de pierre     Un petit singe accroupi, ramassé, sorti d'un œuf de
                          pierre dont deux ou trois gros éclats restent autour.
                          Nu : ni armure, ni bâton, ni coiffe. Le masque pâle aux
                          yeux rouges est déjà là et déjà grand.
                          MASSE : basse et compacte.

  2 · Roi des singes      Debout, le poids sur une jambe, une fourrure jetée sur
                          une épaule, un simple cercle d'or au front. Toujours pas
                          d'armure ni de bâton. Un roi d'animaux, pas du ciel.
                          MASSE : verticale et étroite.

  3 · Sun Wukong          EN L'AIR. Il vient d'apprendre le saut du nuage : corps
                          en diagonale, jambes repliées, un bras tendu devant, la
                          queue qui traîne derrière, une petite volute de nuage
                          sous les pieds. Pas d'armure, pas de bâton.
                          MASSE : diagonale, en vol. Le seul sans appui.

  4 · Sun Wukong          SOUS LA MONTAGNE, et il POUSSE. La montagne remplit le
      sous la montagne    haut du cadre ; lui occupe la moitié basse, une épaule et
                          les deux bras dehors, le corps clairement en effort, le
                          masque haut et calme. L'armure apparaît ICI : une
                          épaulière et le cimier se voient sous la pierre. Un sceau

  CE QUI LES SÉPARE À 24 PIXELS
  1 ramassé · 2 fin · 3 en vol · 4 triangle · 5 vertical sur socle.
  Cinq masses franchement différentes, aucune collision. C'est le seul jury.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Sprite sheet of 5 evolution stages of the same creature, left to right on one row,
evenly spaced.

TRUE 64x64 pixel art, upscaled with nearest-neighbor only. Readable at 24 pixels tall.

SIXTY-FOUR, NOT THIRTY-TWO, AND THIS IS NOT NEGOTIABLE. This creature has a FACE. At
32 the outline alone eats a head: one pixel of outline on each side of a twelve-pixel
skull leaves nothing inside, and the face comes out as one black smear. Draw at 64.

FLAT CARTOON, NEVER AN ILLUSTRATION — read this before anything else:
this is the SAME drawing technique as every other sheet in this game. Only the
REGISTER changes — solemn instead of cute. The technique does not change at all.
- flat blocks of color with one hard outline. Every color is ONE solid tone
- NO shading, NO soft light, NO airbrush, NO glossy highlight, NO reflection
- NO drawn fur, NO surface texture, NO engraved detail, NO fine linework
- hard pixel edges, no anti-aliasing, no blur
- never a painting, never concept art, never a realistic or photographic object

STRICT: maximum 8 flat colors for the whole sheet including the outline.
No texture, no dithering, no noise, no gradients. Simple geometric shapes.
Suggested split, and it is tight on purpose:
  outline / dark fur / mid fur / PALE FACE / red / gold / stone grey / one pale highlight

THE FACE IS THE WHOLE PROBLEM, AND THE SOLUTION IS ALREADY IN THE MYTH.
Sun Wukong wears the opera mask: a PALE face with a red marking around the eyes,
against dark fur. Draw exactly that, and draw it LARGE.
- the pale mask must be a big flat shape, never a few scattered pixels
- the eyes are two dark marks ON the pale mask — light against dark, so they survive
  when every dark tone in the sheet collapses to the same black
- NEVER draw the face in three close dark tones. They will merge and you will have
  drawn a black blob. This exact mistake killed the previous sheet in this project

IDOL REGISTER — what the creature IS:
- adult proportions from stage 2 onward, calm and settled
- NO blush marks. NO wide smile. NO kawaii. This is NOT a mascot
- eyes are NARROW, almond or slit-shaped, calm and aware — never huge round eyes
- mouth closed and neutral
- expression: composed, ancient, faintly imperious. Serene, never friendly, never sleepy
- the pose is ALIVE, never heraldic: the weight sits on one side, the body turns,
  the head is set off the centre line. Never a flat symmetrical emblem, never a logo
- ornament is INSIGNIA, not decoration: bands, plates, a cap, a circlet — drawn as a
  FEW LARGE FLAT SHAPES, never fine engraving, never confetti
- palette deep and rich, with gold used sparingly

THIS PARAGRAPH OVERRIDES THE LINE ABOVE, FOR THIS CREATURE ONLY:
every other idol in this game is already complete at stage 1 and never grows.
SUN WUKONG DOES GROW, and it is the point. He is the only one of his rank born
rather than revealed — out of a stone egg, which in a game called Éclosion is the
reason he is here at all. Stage 1 IS a small creature. Stages 2 to 5 are a real life.

NOTHING BEHIND HIM. NO BACKGROUND FIGURES OF ANY KIND.
An earlier version of this brief asked for crowds of flat silhouettes behind him — soldiers,
monkeys, ranks of Heaven. It was tried and it failed: they read as clutter at full size and
as dirt at 24 pixels, and they stole the eye from the only thing that matters, which is him.
Draw the creature and nothing else. The frame is empty around him.

WHAT MAKES THE FIVE DIFFERENT IS THE SILHOUETTE, NEVER THE DECOR.
Five creatures that differ only by their props look identical, whatever the drawing
quality. This is the most expensive lesson in this project. Change the MASS and the
POSTURE at every stage.

Centered, full body, seen at a slight three-quarter angle — never flat-on.
Depth comes from parts of the body passing IN FRONT of and BEHIND other parts,
with a clean outline at every crossing. Transparent background. No shadow, no ground line,
no background, no text, no frame.
Light and glow are allowed ONLY as one extra FLAT pale shape with a clean edge —
never a blur, never a gradient, and it still counts toward the 8 colors.

Last instruction, and it overrides every word above: 8 flat colors, hard outline,
no shading, no texture. Between grandeur and flatness, choose FLATNESS.

The 5 stages, in order:

1. a SMALL monkey, just out of the stone. He is crouched low and compact, and the broken
   stone egg is still around him — two or three large flat shards, clearly separate from
   his body. Bare, no armour, no staff, no cap. The pale opera mask is ALREADY there and
   already large: it is his identity from the first frame. Narrow eyes. Nothing behind him.

2. STANDING, and the mass changes completely: upright, weight on one leg, a plain cape or
   a fur wrap across one shoulder, a simple flat circlet. No armour yet, no staff. He is a
   king of animals, not of heaven. Nothing behind him.

3. AIRBORNE, and this is the ONLY stage with no support under him. He has just learned the
   cloud somersault: the body is on a clear DIAGONAL, legs folded up, one arm extended
   forward, the tail streaming out behind, and one small flat wisp of cloud under his feet —
   a wisp, not a base. Still no armour, still no staff, still the plain circlet. The mask
   stays calm: he is travelling, not straining.

4. UNDER THE MOUNTAIN, AND HE IS PUSHING. The silhouette inverts and this is the only stage
   where he is not upright. The mountain fills the TOP of the frame — one big flat mass with
   two or three plane changes, no texture, no drawn rocks. He occupies the LOWER HALF and he
   is unmistakably present: one shoulder and BOTH arms out from under the stone, the body
   visibly straining upward, the pale mask high in the frame and perfectly calm. He is
   trapped, never buried — if a viewer reads this as a rock with something under it, the
   stage has failed. This is where the ARMOUR first appears: one flat shoulder plate and the
   phoenix-feather cap show from beneath the mountain. One flat gold seal shape on the stone.

5. AT REST, and it reads as the release of stage 4. Standing on a WIDE flat cloud drawn as
   one simple shape, the staff planted VERTICALLY beside him, armour and cap complete, the
   two long plumes sweeping HIGH. Weight settled, perfectly still. Nothing behind him — the
   cloud alone carries the stage. No roar, no weapon raised, no threat.
